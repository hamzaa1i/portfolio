## Overview

Reve Stitching is a knitted-garment manufacturer in Faisalabad that produces for UK fashion retailers and other international buyers. What began as a request for a company website grew into the business's primary digital operations layer: a public product experience, structured quote and sample intake, internal admin tooling, an ERPNext connection, and a portal where clients follow their own orders.

I build and maintain the platform end to end, working directly with the factory workflows it supports. It runs in production today and is still being extended.

## Context

International buyers evaluate garment manufacturers constantly. Before this platform existed, Reve had no website, no digital brand presence, and no structured way to receive an enquiry. Buyer questions arrived through informal channels, quotes were assembled by hand, and nothing fed into the systems the factory actually ran on.

The commercial requirement was broader than a marketing site. The business needed buyer-facing credibility and operational intake in one place: a public site that presents capability professionally, and a pipeline that turns enquiries into structured, tracked records instead of scattered messages.

## Role

My role covers the platform as a product: architecture, implementation, operations and iteration. I work alongside the people who use it, from the admin team processing quotes to the floor staff whose material movements the ERP side reflects. That closeness to real operations shapes most of the design decisions in this case study.

## System design

The platform is Astro with TypeScript. Public pages render statically; operational routes run on demand. Supabase and a Turso/libSQL database accessed through Drizzle hold intake records and portal data. Resend handles transactional email. Sentry captures errors in production.

The ERPNext integration is deliberately fail-safe. When a quote is submitted, the platform creates a Lead record in the factory's ERPNext instance. The integration runs after the quote is saved, so if ERPNext is unreachable the quote still succeeds and the Lead is reconciled later. Operational systems fail softly; the intake path never breaks because a downstream system blinked.

## Engineering decisions

**Fail-safe over strict consistency.** Every external dependency in the intake path, from email delivery to ERPNext, is wrapped so the buyer's submission succeeds first and back-office sync settles afterwards. For a manufacturer whose enquiries arrive across time zones, the record must never be lost to a transient failure.

**Structured intake at the boundary.** Validation happens with Zod at the API boundary, so everything downstream, admin views, portal displays, ERP records, works against typed, normalized data instead of parsing free text.

**Drafts, not verdicts, for AI.** The AI layers write drafts into the admin view. A person reviews, edits and confirms. This keeps the speed benefit without letting a model make commercial commitments.

## Results

The platform carries live production traffic today. Buyers submit structured quote and sample requests that flow into admin tooling and the ERP. The client portal gives order visibility without ad-hoc status calls. I continue to maintain and extend it as the factory's needs sharpen.
