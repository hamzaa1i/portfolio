## Overview

Haus Couture is a UK-based premium streetwear label. I designed and built its first custom storefront, and later carried the brand onto Shopify, where the store now runs in production at hauscouture.co.uk with a full catalogue and GBP checkout.

This is one project with two technical phases, and the interesting part is the judgement in between: recognizing when a bespoke build stops being the right commerce architecture.

## Phase A: the custom storefront

The label started with a custom Astro website. I designed and built it: information architecture, typography, layout, and responsive implementation, deployed on Vercel.

A bespoke build gives full control over presentation, and for a launch-phase brand that control is worth its maintenance cost. The custom storefront established the visual direction, the product narrative and the catalogue structure while the brand was still finding its shape.

The archived deployment still runs at haus-couture.vercel.app as a record of that first direction.

## The decision

As the commercial requirements became concrete, the picture changed. A production store needs battle-tested checkout, payment handling, tax and policy machinery, fraud tooling and an operational backend a small team can run. Rebuilding and maintaining all of that bespoke, for a catalogue of this size, was engineering effort spent below the waterline: necessary, invisible, and never as trustworthy as a platform that solves commerce for a living.

So the architecture changed. The brand moved to Shopify, and the work moved from building storefront infrastructure to building the store itself.

## Phase B: the production store

On Shopify I own the storefront as a product:

- catalogue architecture: product and variant structure, with four size variants per product across the range
- collection structure: six collections organizing the catalogue
- theme customization and configuration carrying the dark premium visual direction from the custom phase
- responsive product experience, from catalogue to product page to checkout
- policies and content setup
- domain integration and launch

The store runs in GBP with production policies live. The first custom phase was not wasted work: the visual direction, catalogue thinking and product narrative carried straight into the production store.

## What I credit

To be precise about the division of labour: Shopify provides the commerce platform, checkout and payment infrastructure. My work is the storefront and store itself: the original custom implementation, the visual direction, the catalogue and collection architecture, the theme customization, and the launch.

## Results

The store is in production with a responsive catalogue of style families across six collections, GBP checkout, and live commerce policies. The archived custom storefront remains online as a record of the first technical direction.

## Lessons

Building the same store twice taught me to separate two questions early: what makes this brand distinctive, and what is undifferentiated infrastructure. The first deserves custom work. The second usually deserves a platform. Getting that split right is the difference between a launch that scales and one that has to be rebuilt.
